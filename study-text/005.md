## Slide 3

INTRODUCTION
Crohn’s disease is a chronic inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) that causes inflammation of the digestive tract. It can affect any part of the gastrointestinal tract, from the mouth to the anus, but commonly affects the terminal ileum and colon. The inflammation may occur in patches, with healthy areas between affected sections. Crohn’s disease usually has periods of flare-ups, when symptoms become worse, and remission, when symptoms improve or disappear.
DEFINITIONS
CROHN’S DISEASE: A chronic, relapsing inflammatory disease of the gastrointestinal tract caused by an abnormal immune response.
INFLAMMATORY BOWEL DISEASE (IBD): A group of chronic disorders characterized by inflammation of the digestive tract, mainly Crohn’s disease and ulcerative colitis.
FLARE-UP: A period when Crohn’s symptoms and intestinal inflammation become active or worsen.
REMISSION: A period when symptoms are minimal or absent and inflammation is reduced.

## Slide 4

AETIOLOGY
Crohn’s disease has a multifactorial aetiology, meaning there is no single known cause. It results from the interaction of:
Genetic susceptibility : e.g., family history and NOD2 gene variations
Abnormal immune response : excessive immune reaction causes intestinal inflammation
Altered gut microbiome (dysbiosis) : imbalance of normal intestinal microorganisms
Intestinal barrier dysfunction ; increased exposure of immune cells to bacterial substances
Environmental factors : especially smoking, diet, infections, lifestyle, and some medications
Stress : may worsen symptoms but is not a direct cause
Genetic susceptibility + Environmental triggers + Altered microbiome/barrier + Abnormal immune response → Chronic intestinal inflammation → Crohn’s disease

## Slide 5

PATHOPYSIOLOGY
Genetic susceptibility + environmental factors trigger an abnormal immune response to intestinal microorganisms.
Disruption of the intestinal barrier and altered gut microbiome lead to persistent inflammation.
Inflammatory cells release cytokines such as TNF-α, IL-12, and IL-23, causing chronic intestinal inflammation.
The inflammation is transmural (affects all layers of the bowel wall) and occurs in skip lesions.
This results in ulcers, bowel-wall thickening, strictures, fistulas, abscesses, and malabsorption.
Flow:
Genetic & Environmental Factors → Abnormal Immune Response → Chronic Transmural Inflammation → Intestinal Damage & Complications

## Slide 6

TYPES
Ileocolitis (most common): Affects the terminal ileum and colon.
Ileitis: Affects only the ileum.
Crohn’s Colitis: Affects only the colon.
Gastroduodenal Crohn’s Disease: Affects the stomach and duodenum.
Jejunoileitis: Affects the jejunum and ileum.

## Slide 7

SIGN AND SYMOTOMS
Persistent diarrhea
Abdominal pain and cramping
Weight loss and loss of appetite
Fatigue and weakness
Fever
Blood or mucus in stool
Mouth sores
Nausea and vomiting
In severe cases: fistulas, intestinal obstruction, and malnutrition.

## Slide 8

INVESTIGATION
Blood tests: Detect anemia, infection, and inflammation.
Stool tests: Rule out infection and detect intestinal inflammation.
Colonoscopy with biopsy: Examines the colon and confirms the diagnosis.
Endoscopy: Examines the upper gastrointestinal tract.
CT or MRI scan: Detects inflammation, strictures, fistulas, and other complications.
Capsule endoscopy: Helps examine the small intestine.

## Slide 9

MEDICAL MANAGEMENT
Medical Management
Anti-inflammatory drugs and corticosteroids to reduce inflammation.
Immunomodulators and biologic therapy to control the immune response.
Antibiotics when infection or fistulas are present.
Nutritional support and vitamin/mineral supplements.
Surgery for complications such as obstruction, fistulas, or severe disease.

## Slide 10

NURSING MANAGEMENT
Monitor abdominal pain, bowel movements, weight, and vital signs.
Maintain fluid and electrolyte balance.
Encourage adequate nutrition and small frequent meals.
Administer prescribed medications and monitor side effects.
Provide emotional support and educate the patient about disease management

## Slide 11

NURSING DIAGNOSIS
Acute Pain related to intestinal inflammation and abdominal cramping.
Diarrhea related to intestinal inflammation.
Imbalanced Nutrition: Less Than Body Requirements related to poor absorption and loss of appetite.
Deficient Fluid Volume related to frequent diarrhea and vomiting.
Fatigue related to anemia and chronic illness.
Risk for Infection related to immunosuppressive therapy and impaired intestinal integrity.
Anxiety related to chronic illness and changes in lifestyle/body image.

## Slide 12

COMPLICATIONS
Intestinal obstruction – narrowing of the bowel due to inflammation or scarring (strictures).
Fistulas – abnormal connections between the intestine and other organs or the skin.
Abscesses – collections of pus caused by infection.
Malnutrition and weight loss – due to poor nutrient absorption.
Anemia – caused by intestinal bleeding or poor absorption of nutrients.
Perforation – a hole in the intestinal wall.
Increased risk of colorectal cancer – especially with long-standing colonic disease.

## Slide 13

DIFFERENTIAL DIAGNOSIS
Ulcerative colitis
Irritable bowel syndrome (IBS)
Intestinal infections (e.g., tuberculosis or bacterial infections)
Celiac disease
Appendicitis
Colorectal cancer
Diverticulitis
Key point: These conditions may cause similar symptoms such as abdominal pain, diarrhea, weight loss, and bowel changes, so investigations are needed to confirm Crohn's disease.
