## Slide 4

INTRODUCTION
The circulatory system depends on healthy arteries to deliver oxygenated blood and nutrients to the heart and body tissues.
Atherosclerosis is the build up of fats, cholesterol and other substances in and on artery walls.
It is the leading cause of myocardial infarction (heart attack), stroke and peripheral vascular disease worldwide.

## Slide 5

TYPES OF ATHEROSCLEROSIS
Atherosclerosis can be classified according to the type of arterial lesion/plaque:
Fatty streaks: Early lesions caused by accumulation of lipids in the arterial wall.
Fibrous plaque: A raised lesion containing cholesterol, smooth, muscle cells, and fibrous tissue.
Artheromatous plaque: Advanced plaque with lipid-rich core covered by a fibrous cap.
Complicated plaque: Advanced plaque that may rupture, bleed, calcify, or develop a thrombus, potentially causing heart attack or stroke.

## Slide 6

DEFINITION
Atherosclerosis is a specific form of arteriosclerosis characterized by the accumulation of lipids, cholesterol , calcium, cellular waste and fibrous materials within the walls of large and medium-sized arteries forming plaques that narrow and harden the arteries and reduce blood flow.
Simple explanation:
Fat builds up inside an artery > plaque forms > the artery becomes narrow > less blood reaches tissue.

## Slide 8

PATHOPHYSIOLOGY
It develops over many years:
Endothelial injury: High BP , smoking , high glucose and toxins damage the inner lining.
Lipid Entry: LDL “bad cholesterol” enters the damaged wall.
Inflammation: Monocytes become macrophages > ingest LDL > become “foam cells.”
Fatty Streak: Early lesions forms.
Plaque Formation: Smooth muscle cells + collagen + calcium make a fibrous cap over the fatty core.
Arterial Narrowing : Reduced blood flow and oxygen to tissues.
Plaque rupture + Thrombosis: If the cap ruptures, a blood clot forms which can completely block the artery .

## Slide 12

NURSING DIAGNOSIS
INEFFECTIVE TISSUE PERFUSION
Related to: Narrowing of arteries by atherosclerotic plaque .
As evidenced by: Diminished peripheral pulses, cool extremities, leg pain with walking, skin colour changes.
Goal: Patient will maintain adequate peripheral circulation.
ACUTE PAIN
Related to: Reduced blood flow and oxygen to cardiac or peripheral tissues .
As evidenced by: chest pain/angina, leg pain with walking , facial grimacing, guarding.
Goal: Patient will report pain reduced to 0-2/10
RISK FOR ACUTE CORONARY SYNDROME
Related to : Atherosclerotic plaque in coronary arteries and risk for plaque rupture.
As evidenced by : History of CAD , risk factors ( diabetes, smoking, hypertension)
Goal : Patient will remain free of MI
