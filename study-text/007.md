## Slide 2

DEFINITION
GERD is defined as a chronic reflux of stomach content into the oesophagus due to damaged or weakened lower oesophageal sphinter (LES). GERD is also referred to as ‘’acid reflux disease’’

## Slide 3

CAUSES
Weak or dysfunctional LES
Delayed emptying of gastric content due to: increased gastric volume e.g overeating, left sided lying, increased gastric pressure from carbonated beverages and fatty foods, pyloric stenosis.
Age
Stress
Anxiety
Pregnancy
Smoking

## Slide 6

DIAGNOSTIC INVESTIGATIONS
PH MONITORING: Tube placed in the lower oesophagus to measure the PH within 24 hours. PH of the oesophagus is 7 and PH of the stomach is 4. A PH of 5 or less is suggestive of the presence of acid in the esophagus.
Barium swallow
Upper G.I endoscopy

## Slide 7

MEDICAL MANAGEMENT
PROTON PUMP INHIBITORS (PPIs) e.g omeprazole, oesomeprazole, lansoprazole, and pantoprazole.
HISTAMINE 2 RECEPTORS BLOCKERS e.g cimetidine, ranitidine, famotidine, and nizatidine
ANTACIDS e.g magnesium hydroxide (milk of magnesium), calcium carbonate, sodium carbonate.
PROKINETICS e.g metoclopramide
SURGERY

## Slide 8

NURSING AND LIFE STYLE MODIFICATIONS
Advice patient to avoid spicy foods, carbonated beverages, alcohol, smoking etc...
Eat low fat but high fibre diet to increase stomach emptying.
Wearing of loose clothing
Elevate head of the bed to about 6-12 inches (15-20cm) high.
Eat small frequent meals to decrease gastric pressure.
Sit up at least 1 hour after eating before lying down.
Avoid eating right before bed ( last meal should be 3 hours before bed).
