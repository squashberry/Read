## Slide 4

INTRODUCTION
This is an inflammatory process of pancreas and its caused by an inciting event which damages the pancreatic parenchyma, leading to a chain reaction
Pancreatic injury causes enzymes to leak into pancreas resulting in autodigestion leading to more injury and enzyme leakage
This positive feedback cycle causes significant pancreatic edema and fat necrosis

## Slide 5

DEFINITION
Pancreatitis is inflammation of the pancreas that occurs when pancreatic digestive enzymes become activated prematurely and begin damaging the pancreatic tissue.

## Slide 8

PATHOPHYSIOLOGY
Initiating factors
Gallstones (most common) and/or alcohol abuse are the main causes. Other causes: hypertriglyceridemia, drugs, trauma, infections, hypercalcemia, tumors, ERCP.
2. Ductal obstruction or toxic injury
Gallstone blocks the ampulla of Vater → obstruction of pancreatic duct. Alcohol and other toxins cause direct injury to acinar cells and increase protein plugs in small ducts.
3. Premature activation of digestive enzymes
Inside the acinar cells, trypsinogen is prematurely converted to trypsin (instead of in the duodenum).Trypsin then activates other zymogens → phospholipase A2, elastase, lipase, amylase.
4. Autodigestion of the pancreas
Activated enzymes digest pancreatic tissue → inflammation, edema, and necrosis. Phospholipase Az damages cell membranes and blood vessels. Elastase destroys connective tissue; lipase causes fat necrosis.
5. Inflammatory response
Release of cytokines (IL-1, IL-6, TNF-a) → systemic inflammatory response syndrome (SIRS).Increased vascular permeability → fluid shifts, third spacing, and hypovolemia

## Slide 9

TYPES
1. Acute Pancreatitis
Acute pancreatitis is a sudden and rapid swelling of the pancreas. It usually lasts for a short time. Most people get better within a few days, but severe cases can be dangerous.
2. Chronic Pancreatitis
Chronic pancreatitis is a long-term inflammation that gets worse over time. It creates permanent scar tissue in the organ. This damage stops the pancreas from making enough digestive enzymes and hormones, leading to weight loss or diabetes.

SIGNS AND SYMPTOMS
Severe upper abdominal (epigastric) pain
Pain may radiate to the back
Nausea and vomiting
Abdominal tenderness
Fever
Tachycardia
Abdominal distension
Reduced bowel sounds
weight loss

INVESTIGATIONS
Important investigations include:
Serum lipase
Serum amylase
Full blood count
Electrolytes
Liver function tests
Abdominal ultrasound
CT scan when indicated

## Slide 12

NURSING MANAGEMENT
Monitor vital signs closely.
Assess pain regularly.
Administer prescribed analgesics.
Maintain IV access.
Administer IV fluids as prescribed.
Monitor intake and output.
Monitor electrolytes and blood glucose.
Manage nausea and vomiting.
Maintain nutritional support as prescribed.
Observe for complications.

## Slide 13

NURSING DIAGNOSIS
Acute pain related to pancreatic inflammation.
Deficient fluid volume related to vomiting and fluid shifting.
Imbalanced nutrition: less than body requirements related to reduced intake and impaired digestion.
Nausea related to gastrointestinal and pancreatic inflammation.
Risk for unstable blood glucose level related to impaired pancreatic function.
