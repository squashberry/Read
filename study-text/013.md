## Slide 4

Introduction
Shock is a life-threatening medical emergency in which the circulatory system fails to provide adequate blood flow and oxygen to body tissues.
Inadequate oxygen delivery causes cellular injury, organ dysfunction and, if untreated, death.
Early recognition and immediate treatment are essential.

## Slide 5

Definitions
Shock: Acute circulatory failure causing inadequate tissue perfusion.
Tissue perfusion: Delivery of oxygenated blood and nutrients to body tissues.
Hypovolemic shock: Inadequate circulating blood or fluid volume.
Cardiogenic shock: The heart cannot pump enough blood to meet body needs.
Distributive shock: Abnormal vasodilation and distribution of blood.
Obstructive shock: Physical obstruction prevents adequate blood flow.

## Slide 6

Aetiology
Hypovolemic: severe bleeding, trauma, burns, vomiting, diarrhoea, dehydration and excessive fluid loss.
Cardiogenic: myocardial infarction, severe heart failure, cardiomyopathy, arrhythmias and myocarditis.
Distributive: sepsis, severe allergic reaction and spinal cord injury.
Obstructive: pulmonary embolism, cardiac tamponade and tension pneumothorax.

## Slide 7

Pathophysiology
Shock begins when tissue perfusion becomes inadequate, causing reduced oxygen supply to cells. The body initially responds through tachycardia, vasoconstriction, and fluid retention to maintain blood pressure and vital-organ perfusion. If the condition persists, cells develop hypoxia and switch to anaerobic metabolism, resulting in lactic acid accumulation and metabolic acidosis. Continued hypoxia causes cellular damage, impaired organ function, and eventually multiple-organ failure and death

## Slide 8

Types of Shock
1. Hypovolemic shock – loss of blood or body fluids.
2. Cardiogenic shock – failure of the heart to pump effectively.
3. Distributive shock – widespread vasodilation/abnormal blood distribution.
• Septic shock • Anaphylactic shock • Neurogenic shock
4. Obstructive shock – physical obstruction to blood flow.

## Slide 9

Signs and Symptoms
General: tachycardia, hypotension, rapid breathing, weak pulse, pale/cool/clammy skin.
Restlessness, anxiety, dizziness, confusion or reduced consciousness.
Delayed capillary refill, reduced urine output, thirst and weakness.
Severe cases may cause cyanosis and cold extremities.
Septic shock may initially present with warm/flushed skin; anaphylaxis may cause wheezing, swelling and rash.

## Slide 10

Investigations – Assessment
Vital signs: blood pressure, pulse, respiratory rate, temperature and oxygen saturation.
Neurological assessment: level of consciousness.
Peripheral perfusion: capillary refill, skin colour/temperature and pulses.
Fluid status: urine output and fluid balance.
Identify the likely source/cause of shock.

## Slide 11

Investigations – Laboratory & Diagnostic Tests
Full blood count; blood grouping and cross-matching.
Electrolytes, glucose, urea and creatinine; liver function tests.
Arterial blood gas and serum lactate.
Coagulation profile and blood cultures when infection is suspected.
ECG, chest X-ray, echocardiography, ultrasound and CT when indicated.

## Slide 12

Medical Management
Hypovolemic: control bleeding and replace blood/fluid losses.
Cardiogenic: treat the cardiac cause; vasopressors/inotropes or revascularisation may be required.
Septic: appropriate antibiotics, IV fluids when indicated and vasopressors if hypotension persists.
Anaphylactic: IM adrenaline (epinephrine) is first-line; oxygen and IV fluids may be needed.
Obstructive: urgently relieve the obstruction, e.g. tension pneumothorax or tamponade.

## Slide 13

Nursing Management
Assess ABCs and monitor vital signs frequently.
Administer oxygen and prescribed fluids, blood products and medications.
Establish/maintain IV access and monitor ECG and oxygen saturation.
Monitor urine output, fluid balance, skin, pulses and level of consciousness.
Control external bleeding when appropriate.
Keep the patient warm, reduce anxiety, maintain infection control and document response.

## Slide 14

Nursing Diagnoses
Decreased Cardiac Output related to altered cardiac function.
Deficient Fluid Volume related to excessive loss of blood or body fluids.
Impaired Gas Exchange related to inadequate oxygen delivery.
Ineffective Tissue Perfusion related to decreased circulating volume or cardiac output.
Anxiety related to acute illness and threat to life.
Risk for Acute Kidney Injury related to prolonged inadequate renal perfusion.

## Slide 15

Complications
Acute kidney injury.
Acute respiratory distress.
Heart failure and brain injury.
Liver dysfunction and metabolic acidosis.
Disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC).
Multiple-organ dysfunction syndrome (MODS).
Tissue necrosis, cardiac arrest, coma and death.

## Slide 16

Differential Diagnosis
Severe dehydration.
Syncope.
Hypoglycaemia.
Severe anaemia.
Acute myocardial infarction.
Pulmonary embolism.
Severe infection/sepsis.
Adrenal insufficiency, drug overdose, electrolyte disturbances and acute stroke
