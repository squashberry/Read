Causes   of   congenital   heart   diseases Actual   cause   is  unknown Genetic   syndromes   e.g.   Down’s   syndrome,   Turner’s   syndrome,   and the   trisomies. Viruses   e.g.   rubella   e.g.   Patent   Ductus   Arteriosus,   Pulmonary    Stenosis. Maternal   drug   abuse   and   alcohol   use:   30%   of   infants   with   fetal    alcohol   syndrome   also  have   CHD.

Causes   of   CHD   cont’d Poor  maternal   nutrition Maternal   age   above   40. Insulin   dependent   diabetic   mother:   transposition   of the   great   arteries,   ventricular   septal   defect   and   Coarctation   of   the   aorta   are    common   in   diabetic   mothers.

CYANOTIC   CONGENITAL   HEART   CONDITIONS Right   to  left shunt Blood   moves   from   right   side   of the   heart   to  the   left By-passing   the   lungs Therefore ,   non-oxygenated   blood   reaches   body   and   brain Causing   central   cyanosis

Slide 11
CYANOTIC   CONGENITAL   HEART   CONDITIONS Unoxygenated   blood   enters   the   systemic   circulation Oxygen   saturation   85%   or  less. Due  to   mixing   of   oxygenated   and   unoxygenated   blood. Occurs   when   a   heart   defect   allows   large   volume   of   oxygen-poor   blood    in the   right   side   of the   heart, to   mix   with   the   oxygenated   blood   in   the   left  side   of   the   heart.

Tetralogy   of   Fallots-   TOF This   is   the   most common   cause   of   cyanotic  congenital   heart   diseases As   the  name   implies,   there   are   four   cardinal   anatomical	 defects Making   the  newborn cyanotic, babies   often   referred   to  as  “blue   baby”. Four   anomalies   are-   PROV 1.   Pulmonary   stenosis 2.   Right ventricular   hypertrophy 3.   Overriding   aorta. 4.  Ventricular   septal   defect

Slide 15
Tetralogy   of   Fallots-TOF Due   to   pulmonary   stenosis,   there   is   shunting   of   blood   from   the   right    ventricle   to  the  left   ventricle   and   aorta. Therefore,   deoxygenated   blood   is   forced   through   the   septal   defect   to    the   left   ventricle. The   right   ventricular   hypertrophy   develops   due   to   overload.

Slide 16
Signs   and   symptoms   of   TOF Cyanosis   of  lips   and   mucous   membrane-blue   babies. Shortness   of  breath Dizziness,   fainting   or   seizure Weak   pulses Clubbing   of   the   fingers. Polycythemia Squatting;   Easily   get   tired   and   dyspnoeac   after   exertion.   Squatting    help   relief dyspnoea

Slide 17
Diagnosis   of   TOF Physical   examination Blood   examination;   increased   RBC’s   and   HB. X-ray;   reveals   “boot   shaped”   and   right   ventricular   hypertrophy,   and small   size  of pulmonary   artery. ECG;   helps   detect   ventricular   hypertrophy   and   changes   in   cardiac    activity Cardiac   catheterization;   demonstrates   location   and   size   of   the   defect.

Slide 18
Nursing management   of   TOF Oxygen   for   cyanosis Good   nutrition   to   build   good   immunity-   small   frequent,   easily    digestible   food. Antibiotics   for   chronic   infections Limit   activities   to   prevent   exertional   dyspnoea The definitive treatment is surgical repair usually 48 to 72 months of age.

Slide 19
Surgical   treatment   of   TOF Total  repair   done   at  5-6   years  of  age. Blalock   Taussing   shunt   or   procedure :   done   by   surgical   placement   of    an   artificial tube   between   a vessel   from  the   aorta   usually   the    subclavian   artery   and the   pulmonary   artery. This   improves   blood   supply   to  the   lungs. More   useful   for   children   with   cardiac   failure   in   early   childhood. Total   surgical   repair has   a   good   prognosis   and   less   mortality. Corrective:   close   VSD   and   relieve   Pulmonary   Stenosis

Slide 20
TRANSPOSITION   OF   GREAT   ARTERIES The  aorta  is  connected   to   the   right   ventricle, and   the   pulmonary   artery  is   connected   to   the   left  ventricle    (discordant   ventriculo-arterial   connection). Blue   blood   is   returned   to   the   body   and   red   blood   is   returned   to   the    lungs. Occurs   in   infants   with   diabetic   mothers.

Slide 21
Signs   and   symptoms Cyanosis Congestive   heart   failure. Hyperapnoea   due   to   low   arterial   oxygen. Delayed   growth   and   development.

Slide 22
Diagnosis Cardiac   catheterization   to   see   anatomical   defect. echocardiography-   demonstrates   the  abnormal   connection   and    associated  abnormalities. Blood   examination   to   detect   acidosis   and   level   of  cyanosis.

Slide 23
TGA   surgery Neonate:   -   balloon   atrial   septostomy -   corrective   surgery   „arterial   switch“ Child:   corrective   surgery   (arterial  switch   +   VSD   closure)

Slide 24
TRICUSPID   ATRESIA Obstruction   at  the   level   of the   right   atrium   to   ventricle. Valve   that   sends   blood   from   right   atrium   to   right   ventricle   is    closed/blocked. Occurs   commonly   with   pulmonary   stenosis   and   ventricular   septal    defect.

TRUNCUS   ARTERIOSUS Normally   there   are   two   great  arteries   in   the   heart The  aorta  carry  blood   to   the   body. The  pulmonary   artery   carries   blood   to   the lungs. Truncus   arteriosus   occurs   when   there   is   only   one   great   vessel  instead    of   the   two. This   single   great   vessel   carries   blood   both   to   the   body   and   the   lungs.

Slide 28
TRUNCUS   ARTERIOSUS This   single   great   vessel   is   called   truncus. Treatment   is  by   surgery Poor  prognosis. Truncus   arteriosus  is  uncommon.

T O T A L   A N O M A L O U S   P U L M O N A R Y   VENOUS   DRAINAGE The  pulmonary   veins   drain   into   the   right   rather   than   the   left   atrium. If   drainage   is   obstructed,   little   oxygenated   blood   enters   the   right    atrium,   making   the   infant   cyanosed.
