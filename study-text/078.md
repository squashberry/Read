Bronchitis  is an inflammation of the  bronchial tubes , which are the air passages that carry air to and from the lungs This condition causes  coughing , often with  mucus , and may be accompanied by  wheezing, chest discomfort,  and  difficulty breathing .

Slide 3
Types  Acute Bronchitis  – A short-term illness, usually caused by viral infection; common in children. Chronic Bronchitis  – A long-term condition, often caused by smoking or long-term exposure to irritants; rare in children.

Slide 4
Risk factors  Smoking  Exposure to irritant  Respiratory infections Week immune system

Slide 5
Signs and symptoms  Persistent  cough  (initially dry, then productive with mucus) Wheezing Chest discomfort  or tightness Fatigue Fever  (low-grade, not always present) Runny nose  or  sore throat  (early in illness) Shortness of breath  (especially on exertion)

Slide 6
Investigation  History and physical examination FBC Chest x-ray Spirometry  Sputum culture 

Slide 7
Differential diagnosis		 Pneumonia  Asthma TB Lung cancer Heart failure 

Slide 8
Medications: Antibiotics  are usually  not needed  (since it's viral). Inhalers  or  bronchodilators  if wheezing or asthma-like symptoms are present. Cough medicines  are not usually recommended for young children.

Slide 9
Prevention  Immunization  Hygiene Treating respiratory infection

Diphtheria

Slide 11
Diphtheria is a serious bacterial infection that affects the mucous membranes of the throat and nose. Although it spreads easily from one person to another, diphtheria can be prevented through the use of vaccines Has an incubation period of 1 to 10 days 

Slide 12
Sore throat Fever Swollen glands (neck swelling – “bull neck”) Difficulty breathing or swallowing Thick gray or white membrane covering throat and tonsils (hallmark sign) Heart complications (myocarditis) Nerve damage (paralysis) Breathing difficulties

Slide 13
How is Diphtheria Spread Through  respiratory droplets  (coughing, sneezing) Direct contact with  infected sores or ulcers Contaminated  objects or surfaces

Slide 14
Investigations  Throat swab : For bacterial culture and toxin testing Clinical symptoms (especially presence of pseudo membrane in throat) Blood tests, blood culture   ECG (if complications suspected)

Slide 15
Treatment Urgent medical care is critical . Antitoxin : Neutralizes the diphtheria toxin (given as early as possible, DAT) Antibiotics : Such as erythromycin or penicillin to kill the bacteria Isolation : To prevent spread to others Supportive care (oxygen, IV fluids, monitoring for complications)
