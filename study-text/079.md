Introduction Chronic infectious disease caused by  Mycobacterium tuberculosis Commonly affects lungs but can involve other organs Children are highly vulnerable.

Slide 3
Epidemiology High burden in developing countries. Children account for 10–15% of TB cases. Higher risk in HIV, malnutrition, and close contact , WHO’s report 2023.

Slide 4
Transmission Spread through airborne droplets. Coughing, sneezing, talking. Children infected mainly by adults.

Slide 5
Types of TB Pulmonary TB (lungs). Extra-pulmonary TB: TB meningitis Lymphadenitis Miliary  TB Bone TB

Slide 6
Pathophysiology Inhaled bacteria reach lungs. Primary complex formation. Immune response tries to contain. May spread in children.

Slide 7
Risk Factors Close contact with TB patient. HIV infection. Malnutrition. Diabetic  Lack of BCG vaccination. Poor ventilation 

Slide 8
Clinical Features Persistent cough (>2 weeks) Fever  night sweats Weight loss Fatigue

Slide 9
Severe TB in Children TB meningitis: seizures, coma. Miliary  TB: severe illness. Severe  Respiratory distress.

Slide 10
Diagnosis History and physical exam Culture Mantoux test Chest X-ray GeneXpert / sputum test

Slide 11
Treatment Intensive phase: HRZE (2 months) Isoniazid (H) Rifampicin (R) Pyrazinamide (Z) Ethambutol (E) Continuation phase: HR (4 months) Isoniazid + Rifampicin Ensure adherence.

Slide 12
Supportive Care Nutritional support. Treat co-infections. Monitor response.

Slide 13
Prevention BCG vaccination. Contact tracing. Isoniazid preventive therapy.

Slide 14
Nursing Management Monitor symptoms. Ensure drug compliance. Health education. Infection control.

Slide 15
Complications TB meningitis. Growth retardation. Respiratory failure. Multidrug resistant (MDR)  Death if untreated.

Slide 16
3 LEVEL OF PREVENTIONS OF TB IN CHILDREN 

Slide 17
Primary prevention  Goal is to Prevent TB infection before it occurs BCG vaccination at birth → Protects against severe TB (TB meningitis,  miliary  TB) Infection control :  Good ventilation Avoid overcrowding Cough hygiene Early detection and treatment of infectious adults Good nutrition  to strengthen immunity

Slide 18
Secondary Prevention Goal: Detect disease early and treat it before it becomes severe or spread Early detection of the disease  Screening tests Tuberculin Skin Test (Mantoux) Clinical assessment Isoniazid Preventive Therapy (IPT)  for: Children under 5 years exposed to TB HIV-infected children Contact tracing:  identify and screen children in contact with TB cases

Slide 19
Tertiary Prevention Goal: Reduce complications, disability, and death in children with active TB Effective treatment  (e.g., HRZE regimen) Supportive care  (nutrition, oxygen, managing symptoms) Prevention of complications  (e.g., paralysis, brain damage) Follow-up and rehabilitation Follow-up care  to detect relapse or complications
